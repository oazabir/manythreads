# workshop

The place where a person sets a bot up by describing it. They open **Add a bot** on the Bots page and write, in plain words, what the bot should do. A built-in helper looks at what the team really has, the platform grades each thing the brief needs, the person answers one list of questions, and the helper proposes the bot's files. A person with the right approves them; until then nothing exists (PLAN P7-02, P7-03, P7-04; SPEC section 9).

Package: `packages/plugins/workshop`. Manifest: `extends: ['job.register', 'capability.call', 'event.emit', 'event.subscribe', 'hook.pre_egress']`, `emits` five `workshop.*` events (three for re-assessment and replay, two for the terminal), `consumes` the knowledge, runner and eval events listed under re-assessment, `dependsOn: ['teams', 'channels', 'bots', 'proposals', 'knowledge', 'bot-runners', 'gateway-llm', 'gateway-mcp', 'repo-git']` (`hook.pre_egress` is for the terminal, [below](#the-terminal-into-a-sandbox-p7-09)). The data-loss guard (`guardrails-dlp`) is deliberately **not** a dependency: without it the Workshop still loads and only the terminal is unavailable (it says so in plain words and never stores a record unchecked). Migrations `workshop/0001_setup_sessions.sql` (`app.setup_sessions`, one person's conversation, `rls: person`), `workshop/0002_workshop_sessions.sql` (`app.workshop_sessions`, the Workshop tab's conversations, owner only; a conversation's title is its first words, cut at a word boundary and never in the middle of a word, unless the first word alone is longer than the limit), `workshop/0004_bot_reassessments.sql` (`app.bot_reassessments`, the notices; team members read, the system writes) and `workshop/0005_terminal_sessions.sql` (the terminal's table; 0003 is the setup-retries migration). Schemas: `packages/shared/src/entities/setup.ts`, `packages/shared/src/api/setup`, `packages/shared/src/bot-md/setup-profile.ts`. The helper's profile: `templates/_setup/PROFILE.md`. The screen: `clients/web/src/screens/setup`.

The same plugin also runs the **Workshop tab** of a bot (P7-05, P7-06): three modes (Build, Rehearse, Live) that work on a bot that already exists, and a learning loop that turns a correction into a lesson, a test case and, when it repeats, a rule. It is described under [The Workshop tab](#the-workshop-tab-p7-05-p7-06) below. It also runs the **Replay** of a bot's earlier guard and gate decisions and the **re-assessment** notices (P7-07), described under [Replay and re-assessment](#replay-and-re-assessment-p7-07). A list of earlier setup conversations on the bot's page is not built.

## The flow

1. **Brief.** `POST /api/teams/:slug/setup/sessions` `{ brief, name? }`. Anyone who may post in the team may start one (the same right as proposing a change to its files). The row is written and a background job is queued; the answer is `201` at once. At most three conversations per person are open (working or waiting for answers); the check runs under a lock on the person, so starting several at once cannot slip past it. A `working` conversation the helper has not written for ten minutes is turned into `failed` ("The helper stopped answering…") whenever its owner reads or starts one, so a stuck one neither hangs the screen nor counts against the limit.
2. **Discovery.** The job runs the helper. It calls `describe_self`, then `ask_clarification` with *every* thing the brief needs. The platform grades each (below) and asks one question for each gap, all in one list. The conversation becomes `asking`.
3. **Answers.** `POST .../:sessionId/answers` `{ answers: [{ questionId, text }] }` must answer every open question. The job continues the same conversation. At most six rounds of questions.
4. **Proposal.** The helper calls `propose_config`, and the platform refuses until `ask_clarification` has been called at least once in the conversation (a brief with nothing missing is still graded, with an empty gap list, by that call). The platform checks the files (refusals below). If they pass, the files are filed as ordinary proposals ([proposals.md](./proposals.md)): `bots/<slug>/BOT.md`, optionally `skills/<slug>/SKILL.md` and one `TEAM.md` change. The conversation becomes `proposed`.
5. **Approve.** The web screen shows the diff, the checks and "What it won't be able to do". **Approve** applies the proposals in order (team file, skills, the bot last) through the usual approve route. Adding a bot needs a workspace admin ([proposals.md](./proposals.md)); a member or lead sees the proposal and who approves it, and it waits in Proposals. The grants a bot has are the ones its BOT.md names; the bots loader applies them when the file lands. Nothing else applies grants.

`GET .../:sessionId` returns the conversation (the screen polls it while `working`). `POST .../:sessionId/cancel` stops it. A session belongs to the person who started it: nobody else (a lead or admin included) can read, answer or cancel it (`404`).

States: `working` (the helper is thinking), `asking`, `proposed`, `failed` (one plain sentence in `message`), `cancelled`.

**Try again.** `POST .../:sessionId/retry` takes a `failed` conversation back to `working` and runs its last round again from what is stored (the conversation so far and every answer the person gave), so nothing is asked twice and the session id stays the same. It is refused (`409`) for a conversation that is not `failed`, one that already filed its proposals, when the person already has three open, and after **three tries on the same conversation** (`SETUP_RETRY_LIMIT`; the count is the `retries` column, migration `0003_setup_retries.sql`): the sentence says it "keeps stopping" and to start a new one, so a model that fails every time cannot be re-run forever. Only the owner can call it (`404` for anyone else). The screen's failed state offers **Try again** next to **Start again**. If the stored conversation ends with the helper's own words, the job adds one short nudge ("Please continue with the tools.") because a model provider refuses to continue from an assistant turn.

**Withdraw.** The helper files the proposals in the asker's name, and the proposals service lets a person decline only what somebody else wrote. So the screen offers the author **Withdraw** (the proposals' own withdraw route) next to **Review in Proposals**; it is announced like the other actions and focus returns to the heading.

## Why the helper has its own small loop

The task allowed "the model runtime or the Hermes path, whichever the seams allow". Neither can host this session:

* The `model` runtime of [bot-runners.md](./bot-runners.md) (P19-10) serves a **bot's run**: it needs a bot row, a runner assignment, a run, and the bot's grants. While a bot is being set up none of them exists, and inventing a throwaway bot to borrow them would put an actor in the roster that nobody approved.
* A Hermes pod is a long-lived process for a bot; it needs a bot, a pod and a pairing. Same problem, and slower.

So the plugin runs its own loop (`src/loop.ts`, about 100 lines). It is a third agent loop in the code base next to the `model` runtime and Hermes; that is a **proposed** decision for the owner, recorded under "Owner decisions" in [conventions](../engineering/conventions.md). Code is not shared with the model runtime: only the turn, tool call, append skeleton (about 30 lines) is common, while the runtime is an async generator over bot run events that ends only on text and needs a bot, a runner and a run, and this loop ends on special tools, saves its history at every step, nudges a model that forgot to call a tool, and checks for a cancel. Sharing would need an adapter bigger than the overlap: ask the model for a turn, run the tools it asked for, give it the answers, go on until a tool ends the turn. It goes through the **same model door** as every other model call, `provider.llm.chat` of [gateway-llm.md](./gateway-llm.md): the pre-call guard (DLP, budget), the alias and its fallbacks, the spend line. `chat` accepts no bot and no run for this purpose (`botId: null`, `runId: null`, `sessionId`, `purpose: 'setup'`); the spend line carries the session id, and no bot. A workspace without a connected model gets `503` when starting, and a plain sentence if it goes away meanwhile.

The loop is bounded: `limits.steps` of the profile (8) model calls per round, four tool calls per step, 4096 output tokens and, per model call, 240 seconds by default (`MANYTHREADS_WORKSHOP_MODEL_TIMEOUT_MS`, kept between 10 and 540 seconds; found live: real turns took 80 to 230 seconds, so the old two minutes ended sessions that were still working; `src/limits.ts`), 24000 characters of a tool answer (`describe_self` is trimmed to fit, see below). A model that answers in plain words twice instead of calling a tool is given up on (`failed`).

## The profile and the tools

`templates/_setup/PROFILE.md` is the helper's definition: name, role, model aliases (`smart`, `fast`; the first one the workspace has bound is used), `limits.steps`, the tool list and the prompt. It is a platform file: the loader of team templates skips directories that start with `_`, and the plugin reads it through `ctx.templates.setupProfile()`. Frontmatter is checked by `SetupProfile` in shared; the tool list can only leave tools out of the fixed four.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `describe_self` | The team's inventory **as the asker may see it**: bots, knowledge bases, skills, runners the team may use, models by alias, channels, role tags, the tools a bot could be given (and which can change things), the triggers, what the team allows (`shell`, `files`, `browser`), the default guard, and what this person may grant. Names and counts only. Never an address, key, token or message text, and never another team. The policy fields come first (the asker, `runtimeAllowed`, `guardDefault`, the triggers, connections) and are never cut; when a team is big the long lists (bots, capabilities, channels, ...) are halved until it fits and the answer says what was left out. Channels are the team's public ones only. |
| `knowledge_search` | The read-only `knowledge.search` capability, as the person, with the team fixed by the session. Short passages (400 characters), marked as data. |
| `ask_clarification` | Lists every requirement, has the platform grade them, stores the grades, writes one question per gap and **ends the turn**. |
| `propose_config` | Checks the files and, if they pass, files the proposals. Ends the turn. |

Every tool runs as the **person who asked** (never a bot, never the system), in that person's own transaction, so what a tool shows or files is what that person may see or file. The helper has no actor of its own, no grants and no way to apply anything. Tool calls reach the platform's capabilities through `capability.call` ([security.md](./security.md)), not through the wide run door; the manifest lists the only two the plugin may call (`calls: ['knowledge.search', 'proposals.propose']`) and the kernel refuses any other.

## Grading (P7-03)

The grade is decided by code (`src/grade.ts`), from the requirement the helper wrote (`kind`, `name`) and the inventory. The model chooses what the brief needs; it does not choose how it is graded.

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `covered` | The team has it and the bot may simply be given it: a tool that cannot change things, a knowledge base, a skill, a model alias that is bound, a runner the team may use, one of the five triggers that need no connection. |
| `grantable` | It exists and a person with enough standing can switch it on, which is part of what is approved: a tool that can change things (the bot must ask a person each time) when the asker is a lead or admin; a runtime tool the team allows, same asker. |
| `missing` | The team does not have it: a connection (none exist in this release), a knowledge base, a skill, an `inbox` or `webhook` trigger (they need a connection). |
| `blocked` | The rules do not allow it: a shell, files or browser the team has not allowed; a runner the team may not use; anything dangerous for a member. |
| `unclear` | The brief does not say enough, or names something that does not exist. |

`missing`, `blocked` and `unclear` are **gaps**. The platform writes one question per gap (the helper may word it and suggest up to four options), numbered `q1`, `q2`, ... continuing across rounds. `propose_config` refuses to go on while a gap has not been asked about. **A later listing never loosens an earlier verdict**: when the helper lists a requirement again, the stricter grade stays, a gap never turns into `covered`, and a different thing listed under a taken id is kept under a new id. Grades are code, so a model that reworded a missing connection as a note cannot hide it. What cannot be done is written to the proposal's note and shown as "What it won't be able to do".

**Plain words only.** A person never reads an internal name. The reasons, the platform's own questions and the lines of "What it won't be able to do" are built from labels (`src/labels.ts`: `messages.post` reads "post messages", `channel_message` reads "a new message appears in a channel"; a name the table does not know is spelled out with spaces), and a requirement of kind `other` is shown by the sentence the helper wrote, never by the id it chose. A question the helper words itself must contain a question mark, have one to four short choices, and show no internal name (`questionProblem`); the first time one does not, `ask_clarification` is sent back once to reword it, and after that the platform's own question for that gap is used, which always has choices and ends "What should we do?".

**Answered gaps.** A gap the person answered reads **"You decided: <their answer> (<what it was>)"**; one nobody answered stays "Not settled", "Not available yet" or "Not allowed". Only a *recorded* answer counts: answers are written by the route from the questions the platform asked, each names the requirement it was about, and a requirement the helper lists again under a new id has no answer yet. The model cannot mark a gap answered, and an answer never changes a grade (`limitsOf(graded, answers)`).

## What `propose_config` refuses (P7-03)

Code, in `src/policy.ts`. Each refusal is a plain sentence the helper reads and fixes; nothing is filed unless the whole proposal passes.

* The bot's file must load (`parseBotMd`), be an `agent` on the `hermes` runtime, and have a new slug (not one the team has, not `new`, which is the address of the Add a bot page).
* **The text lines of the front matter are generated, not copied** (`src/frontmatter.ts`; found live: a role written unquoted as `Reads posts in #dev and replies` is stored cut at `#`, because YAML reads `#` as a comment). `propose_config` takes the bot's `name` and `role` as plain arguments; the platform drops whatever `name:` and `role:` lines the model wrote and writes fresh quoted ones (the YAML library picks the quote style: double quotes by default, single quotes when the text itself holds double quotes), then reads the file back with `parseBotMd` and refuses with a plain sentence if the parsed name or role differs from what was proposed. Every other line that holds free text (`description`, `title`, `summary`) is read back too: if a plain scalar would be stored differently (a `#` comment, a number, a list) the proposal is refused. A role is one line of at most 200 characters. The same read-back runs on `propose_bot_change` in the Workshop.
* **Never looser than the team default.** The guard preset must be the team's default or stricter (`standard` default: any preset; a stricter default: that preset or `air-gapped`). The default is the `defaults.guardPreset` key of `TEAM.md`; an absent key means `standard`, but a key that is **there and cannot be read** (a typo such as `lax`, a broken front block, `botRuntime` that is not a list) reads as the strictest (`air-gapped`, nothing allowed) and the helper is told to ask a lead to fix the block. A mistake must never loosen a limit.
* **Never more than the person may grant.** A tool that can change things needs `approvals.needsApproval` for it **and** a lead or admin asker. Shell, files and browser (`capabilities.runtime`) only if the team allows them (`defaults.botRuntime` in `TEAM.md`; absent means none) **and** a lead or admin asker. A memory bank is only the team's own, with write access for a lead or admin. A bot for the whole workspace (`visibility: workspace`) is an admin's call.
* **Only a runner the team may use.** `placement.runs_on` must be in the runner catalog for the team, and a bot that needs more than a model call is refused a runner that only calls a model (the same `botPlacementProblem` the loader asks). The old placements (`gateway`, `node`, `runner`) are refused.
* Tools must exist and be named one by one (no wildcards); no `connections` (none exist); no `inbox` or `webhook` trigger; knowledge bases, skills and model aliases must exist (a skill may be created by the same proposal); no `subscription` model (an admin's choice); approvers and handover roles must be role tags of the team, never a named person; `mayTag` must name bots that exist; no evals.
* **Triggers.** A `channel_message` trigger needs `source.channels` (without it the router would answer every message in every public channel), and any channel a `mention` or `channel_message` trigger names must be one of the team's public channels the asker can see (the router fires only in public channels, never in private ones or direct messages).
* **One proposal per file.** A skill listed twice in one call, or a bot or skill path that already has an open proposal, is refused.
* A `TEAM.md` change must come from a lead or admin, be valid, change something, and **cannot change `defaults.botRuntime` or `defaults.guardPreset`**: the helper can never widen what it is allowed to propose.

The refusal text never repeats a name the person may not see (a runner the team may not use is named only in the sentence about itself).

## The Workshop tab (P7-05, P7-06)

On a bot's conversation page (`/t/:team/bots/:botSlug/chat`) there is a **Chat | Workshop** pair of tabs. `?view=workshop` opens the Workshop, `?mode=build|rehearse|live` the mode and `?ws=<id>` the conversation, so reloading comes back to the same place. A Workshop conversation is **the person's own**: `app.workshop_sessions` has one row per conversation with an owner-only row policy (nobody else, a lead and an admin included, reads or continues it; a plain `404`). The entries (what the person wrote, what the helper or the rehearsed bot answered, each tool step, each proposal filed, each refusal) are kept on the row, so a rehearsal transcript is stored there and nowhere else.

Routes (`packages/shared/src/api/workshop`, all under `/api/teams/:slug/bots/:botSlug/workshop/sessions`): `POST` creates one in a mode (optionally with the first message), `GET` lists the person's own for this bot, `GET /:id` reads one (the tab polls while `working`), `POST /:id/messages` `{ text, about? }` sends a message, `POST /:id/end` closes it. Like the setup helper, a message only writes the row and queues the job `workshop.session` (`src/wsession.ts`; detached, one attempt, serialised per session); the model is never called inside a request. Build and Live use the same model aliases as the Add a bot profile (the first one that is bound); Rehearse uses the bot's own.

**Limits** (enforced in code, `shared/src/entities/workshop.ts`): three open conversations per person in all bots (`409`, counted under a lock on the person), 30 messages and 200 entries per conversation, 8 model calls per message and 6 tool calls per call (`src/turn.ts`), 2000 characters per message, 240 seconds per model call (as above). A `working` conversation nobody wrote for ten minutes is turned into `failed` by the same sweep as the setup helper's. Build and Live need the right to post in the team; Rehearse needs a team lead or an admin and an active bot.

### Build

The Add a bot idea applied to a bot that exists. Tools: `describe_self`, `knowledge_search`, `read_bot`, `propose_bot_change`, `record_correction`, `propose_rule`, `propose_compaction`. `propose_bot_change` files a change to `bots/<slug>/BOT.md` as a P7-01 proposal using the **same checks as `propose_config`** (`checkBotEdit` in `policy.ts`: never looser than the team default, never more than the person may grant, tools and models must exist, the front-matter read-back above). The slug is fixed by the bot being edited. Every file goes through `proposals.propose` as the person, so a lead or admin applies it, and a new file under `bots/` (a lessons file, a scenario set) needs an admin, as for any proposal. Nothing is applied by the helper.

### Rehearse

Runs the bot's real instructions (the body of BOT.md), its knowledge and its read tools, on the bot's own model, **with every write tool intercepted by the platform, not by asking the model**. The job opens a *sandbox* on the MCP gateway (`openSandbox`, `sandboxTools`, `sandboxCall`, `closeSandbox` in [gateway-mcp.md](./gateway-mcp.md)). The run is a rehearsal run (`bot_runs.rehearsal`), every tool that is not a read-only one is held and logged in `rehearsal_intercepts` (where it aimed, never what it would have written), a name that was not offered is refused by the turn loop and again by the broker, and the stub write tool `test.write` exists only in a sandbox so there is always something to hold. The tab badges every held step **not really done**. Nothing leaves the sandbox: no message, page, memory, repo write or approval. The run is not counted among the bot's real runs. The reply is labelled "a test answer, nothing was sent". The spend lines carry the bot, `purpose: 'rehearsal'` and `session_id = 'rehearsal:<conversation>'`. The tab says "Rehearsal spend: ...", and the Spend page shows it as a row of its own ("Rehearsal of <bot>"), apart from the bot's real work. It is shown apart, **not budgeted apart**: a bot's own budget counts every line that has the bot's id, so rehearsal lines count toward it. Build, Live and setup lines carry no bot id (the asker is the person, not the bot), so they count only toward the team and workspace budgets ([gateway-llm.md](./gateway-llm.md)). The tests use a hostile scripted model that tries every write it can think of and assert the exact outcome of each.

### Live

Questions about the bot's real runs. The tools `run_stats`, `recent_runs` and `run_detail` read through the **asker's** transaction, so they count only the runs that person may see (row policy), leave out rehearsals, and return how a run started and ended and what it read (kind and reference), never message text. It reads the run records, not the admin audit table. Read only: no tool in this mode can change anything.

### The learning loop (P7-06)

When a person corrects the bot in Build, or says "make that a rule", the helper calls `record_correction`. The platform (`src/learning.ts`, `src/learn-plan.ts`; plain code over text) decides the rest. One batch, filed as ordinary proposals:

1. **A lesson** in `bots/<slug>/lessons.md` (the memory plugin's lessons file): one line, `- <date>: <text>`, at most 400 characters. A lesson that is already in the file (the same by the repeat definition below, so told again in other words too) is not written a second time: the line that says it keeps its date and gets the count `(told N times)` instead, and the proposal says so. When several lines already say it, the count goes on the **oldest** of them, and it stops at 999.
2. **An eval case** in the bot's scenario set (`bots/<slug>/evals/<set>.yaml`, YAML, so the default is `bots/<slug>/evals/lessons.yaml`; the P7-08 `BotEvalCase` schema; the set BOT.md names under `evals.set`, else `lessons`), keyed by a hash of the whole normalised lesson (spacing, case and punctuation do not matter; every word does, "not" included), and not added when the set already holds a case made for a lesson that says the same thing by the rule below (`hasCaseForLesson`), so a lesson told again in other words never adds a second copy of its test, while an opposite lesson adds its own; a case must assert something (`mustMention` or `mustNotMention`). When BOT.md names no set yet, a third proposal adds the `evals: { set, minScore: 0.8 }` block.
3. **A rule**, only when the person asked **in their own words**: the platform looks in the person's latest message for "make that a rule" and its variants (`asksForRule`: a verb such as make, turn, add, save, record or file, then "that", "this" or "it", then "a rule"); the model's say-so is never taken. A negation in the same sentence just before it ("please don't make that a rule", "never make that a rule", "do not turn this into a rule") means the opposite, and a sentence that only mentions rules ("keep the rules short") is not a request. A rule is appended under `## Rules from the Workshop` in BOT.md's instructions and goes through the same BOT.md checks as any change.
4. **A rule for a repeated lesson.** A lesson *repeats* when it is the same as one already in `lessons.md` by this deterministic definition: lower-case the words, drop stop words, cut plural and past-tense endings crudely, and compare the two word sets. They are the same lesson when the shared words are at least **60 percent of all the words either uses** (Jaccard similarity 0.6, `REPEAT_SIMILARITY`); sets of fewer than three words are the same only when equal. **A negation counts**: words such as not, never, don't, without, no and stop are kept as a marker, and a lesson that has one is never the same as one that has none ("use metric units" and "never use metric units" are different lessons, however many words they share). Letters of any script are compared as words. When the same lesson comes up again the helper proposes the rule itself.
5. **Compaction.** When `lessons.md` passes `memory.maxLines` of BOT.md (default 200, the same key as the memory file's), a further proposal replaces it with a shorter one (`propose_compaction`): lessons that are the same are merged into the newest wording marked "(told N times)", then the oldest are dropped until the file is under three quarters of the limit, and the proposal's text **names every lesson it drops**. The platform only rewrites what it can read: from the first lesson on, every line must be a `- ` lesson or blank. A file with a hand-written paragraph, an indented continuation or a `*` bullet there is **not compacted** (the rewrite would delete it without a word), and the helper says which line stopped it. If the header alone is too long, a person must shorten the file by hand and the helper says so.
6. **Refusals in code.** A lesson or rule that would loosen a check is refused with a plain sentence and **nothing is filed**: telling the bot to skip, bypass, ignore or turn off an approval, guard, limit, budget, review or the data-loss filter, to act "without asking", to approve its own work, to put a password, key or token in a message, or to use a tool that needs a person's approval "automatically" (`contradiction`). A rule that is already in BOT.md is not filed twice.

**What the contradiction check is, and is not.** It is a check on *words*, deliberately cautious, and it reads **English only**. It looks for a word that sets a check aside near the name of a check, and a negation just before it ("never skip the review", "don't ignore the guard", "do not go ahead without asking") turns the refusal off, because that sentence keeps the check. Text written mostly in another script, or a word that mixes scripts (a look-alike letter in the middle of "ignore"), is **refused with the reason "the checks can only read English words"**, not passed: the check never says yes to what it cannot read. It does not understand sentences. A paraphrase that uses none of its words ("treat the checklist as optional") passes it. That is by design a first filter and not the guard: every file the helper writes is still a proposal that a person reads and approves, and the approval, not this check, is what keeps a loosened rule out of the bot. A person rewrites a lesson it refuses.

The Workshop, not the proposals service, keeps to **one open proposal per file** for its own filings: before it files, it takes a lock on `team:path` for each file (a Postgres advisory transaction lock, `workshop.file:<team>:<path>`, taken in path order) and then checks that no proposal on that path is still waiting, so two conversations correcting the same bot at the same moment cannot both file (the second is refused in a sentence: the files "already have a proposal waiting for a person"). The batch is filed all together or not at all (one transaction); the transcript's "proposal filed" lines are written only after that commit, so a batch that failed half way shows none. If the person ends the conversation while a tool is still filing, the transcript is patched afterwards to show exactly the proposals that exist. Nothing in this plugin writes to the repo directly: a person's approval is the only way a lesson, a case or a rule reaches the bot. The model never decides that a rule was asked for, that a lesson repeats or that a gap was answered.

## Replay and re-assessment (P7-07)

Two things that answer "is this bot still safe to leave running?" without calling a model. Both sit in the Workshop tab's **Live** mode (`clients/web/src/screens/workshop/Replay.tsx`).

### What is recorded

Before P7-07 the platform kept the held gate decisions (as approvals) and the audit rows of refused calls, but nothing for a decision that **passed**, and not the input of any of them. So the replay has its own record: `app.run_decisions` (migration `bots/0020_run_decisions.sql`, see [bots.md](./bots.md)), one row per decision of a run, **content-blind**: the kind (`gate`, `guard`, `tool`), the stage that decided (`exact`, `regex`, `classifier`, `classifier_unavailable`, `none` for the gate), the outcome as the check itself words it (gate `passed`, `held`, `refused`; guard `allow`, `redact`, `require_approval`, `block`; tool `allowed`, `needs_approval`, `denied`; the replay maps them to allowed, waits for a person or refused), a few facts (the capability name, the guard preset, the rules that fired, the path or the message id) and **never** message text, tool arguments or a matched snippet. A run keeps at most 300 lines. Readers: whoever may read the run (`bot_runs` row policy). Writers: the runtime ([runtime-hermes.md](./runtime-hermes.md), the gate and the guard before the model), the tool door ([gateway-mcp.md](./gateway-mcp.md), every tool call and every guard check on its answer) and the kernel's `ctx.runs.logDecision`, which is the only way a plugin can add a line.

### The free replay

`GET /api/teams/:slug/bots/:botSlug/replay?days=N` (`N` is 1 to 30, default 30; the screen offers 7, 14 and 30; `src/replay.ts` reads, `src/replay-eval.ts` decides; the second is plain code with no database). It takes up to 200 runs of the window and asks **today's** rules the same question for each recorded decision:

* a **gate** decision: today's list of topics that need a person (`guard.gate` of the BOT.md at the head of the team's files) against the message as it reads now (read through the viewer's own access, so a message in a channel the viewer cannot read is simply not checked);
* a **guard** decision: today's preset of the workspace against the preset recorded. Replay is by preset (strict, standard, customer-facing and so on), not by the detectors inside it;
* a **tool** decision: the capability broker is asked again through `ctx.capabilities.preview`, which makes exactly the decision `call` would make and leaves **no audit row and no side effect**.

Each recorded decision comes out as `same`, `tighter` (it went through, now it would wait or be refused), `looser` (the other way round) or `not replayed`, with a plain sentence of why. What cannot be asked again is said so: the answer of the **local model** (the second stage of the gate), the content scan of the data-loss filter, budgets and rate limits, and a detector-level choice. The page counts them under "cannot be checked again" and never as "same".

**Limits, said out loud.** One replay reads at most 6000 decisions (the newest) and asks the broker at most 80 *different* tool questions. When either limit is hit the answer carries `decisionsCapped: true` and a first note that says so ("Only the newest 6000 decisions…", "Not everything was checked: … at most 80 different requests…"), and the screen shows the same sentence above the list. A tool decision past the 80 is listed as "not checked: this replay already asked about too many different requests", never as a failed permission check. A tool decision whose record was kept in part (more than five places, a place cut at 200 characters, a path that was not shaped like a path of the team's files, or a detail over 1500 bytes) is stored with `truncated: true` and replays as **not replayed**: it is never judged as same, tighter or looser. A message counts as "edited since" against the **earliest** time a gate decision read it.

**Free and read only, by construction.** The route is registered with `readOnly: true`, a route option the server honours by opening its transaction with `BEGIN READ ONLY`; a write the handler tries fails with a database error (25006), and the SDK's statement filter refuses what could undo that: session settings (`SET`, `set_config`) and statements that end or restart the transaction (`COMMIT`, `END`, `ABORT`, `BEGIN`, `START TRANSACTION`, `PREPARE TRANSACTION`, a plain `ROLLBACK`). The filter is a stopgap, not a sandbox ([security.md](./security.md)): the guarantee holds for plugin code that goes through the guarded transaction, which is every plugin. No model is called. A test counts the rows of every table the replay could touch and the calls to the model before and after: none change.

**Who sees what.** A team lead or an admin sees every run of the bot they may read; a member sees the runs they started. A lead does **not** read another person's private conversation with the bot (the `bot_runs` row policy, unchanged): those runs are not in a lead's replay and a full replay of one is a `404`. The cost line is shown to every lead (it is a sum of the original run's tokens and price, the ledger itself is admin-only and read by the system for those four totals only).

### The full replay

`POST /api/teams/:slug/bots/:botSlug/replay/runs/:runId/full` `{ confirm?: { tokens, token } }`. A lead or an admin, one run at a time, for a run that recorded a message id (earlier runs have none and are refused with `409`). The first call answers `needs_confirmation` with the **estimate**: the model calls, the read and written tokens and the price of the original run (from the spend ledger; "no price is recorded" when it has none), the cap of the rehearsal (`WORKSHOP_MAX_STEPS` calls) and a sentence that the run is a rehearsal. Nothing runs. The estimate carries a `confirmToken`, a short server hash of the run, the person and the token count; the second call must send back both the number and that token, and anything else (a bare number, a zero, another run's token) is a `409` ("the estimate changed… or it was not the one you were shown"). A run with no recorded spend has an estimate of 0 tokens and still needs its token. The server also refuses (`409`) a message that was **edited after the bot read it**, because asking again would be asking something else. Then the server reads the original message as the viewer sees it **now**, starts a **Rehearse** conversation with it as the first line (`startSession` of the Workshop) and the bot answers in the sandbox of the MCP gateway where every write is held back and marked "not really done". The body is strict: a `text` field is a `400`, the message is never taken from the request. The event `workshop.replay.started` carries ids and counts, no text. This is the seam; it is **not** a batch replay of many runs (see Not built).

### Re-assessment

When something a bot depends on changes, the bot gets one **notice** (`app.bot_reassessments`) with a headline and a list of things to look at again, in fixed text, from subscribers of the platform's own events (`src/reassess.ts`, as the system role). No model is called, so a runner that flaps costs nothing. A notice is filed once while one is open for the same bot, reason and subject.

| Reason | Event | Bots affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| knowledge base added or removed | `knowledge.base.created`, `knowledge.base.deleted` | bots of the team whose BOT.md lists the base under `knowledge:`, and bots that list none (a bot with no list searches every base of its team; the notice says so) |
| runner added, removed, switched off, limited, changed | `bot_runner.runner.created`, `.deleted`, `.updated`, `bot_runner.availability.changed` | bots of the workspace whose BOT.md names the runner (`placement.runs_on`); a label change alone does not count |
| default runner changed | `bot_runner.default.changed` | bots that name no place at all |
| test score dropped | `bots.eval.run.finished` | the bot, when its newest run scored lower than the run before it **of the same set and the same mode** (a free score is never compared with a model score); a rise, a first run and a run that is only under the minimum file nothing |
| connection added or revoked | none yet | Phase 8 has no connection events. The reasons and their texts exist, so the events plug in with one subscription |

`GET /api/teams/:slug/bots/:botSlug/reassessments` (team members read; the Workshop tab shows "N things to check again" as one line in Build and Rehearse and as the full list, with a close button for a lead, in Live) and `POST .../reassessments/:id/close` `{ answer: 'done' | 'dismissed' }` (a lead or an admin; a second close is `404`). Events (registered with schemas in `packages/shared/src/events`): `workshop.reassessment.created`, `workshop.reassessment.closed`, `workshop.replay.started`.

## The terminal into a sandbox (P7-09)

A workspace admin can open a **terminal** (a text window where you type commands) into a throwaway test sandbox of one bot, to debug how it behaves while it is tested. It is reached from the **Workshop tab** of the bot's page (an admin sees "Open a terminal"; the address `?view=workshop&terminal=1`). A member sees nothing, and the server refuses them anyway. The code is `packages/plugins/workshop/src/terminal/`; the page is `clients/web/src/screens/workshop/terminal/`.

### What a sandbox is, in code

`placementOfRun` (`packages/shared/src/entities/terminal.ts`) is the one definition. A run is a **sandbox** only when its own row says `rehearsal = true` **and** it was opened as a `conversation` (the Workshop's Rehearse mode and the bot page's Test action open exactly that, and nothing else sets the flag; the table's own check `bot_runs_rehearsal_conversation` stops any other combination from being stored). **Every other run is production**, and anything the function cannot tell is production too: a webhook, a schedule, a mention, a task, a channel message, an inbound mail, a run a Hermes host gateway or a team pod is serving, a runner's job. A terminal is refused for all of them, by name, with no override. `POST .../terminal` takes an optional `runId`; a run that is not a sandbox answers `403` ("That is a real run, not a test…") and is left running, untouched.

### Where the shell runs, and what it can reach

**On the server host, in a jail built for that one session. It does not run inside a bot's real placement** (the Hermes host gateway, a team pod, a runner), because a rehearsal has none: a rehearsal run is the model answering through the gateway with every write held back, and it has no filesystem. The terminal's sandbox is therefore a new, empty folder plus an isolated shell, which is what an admin debugging a test can honestly use.

The jail is made by an embedded Python helper (`pty-helper.ts`) that needs Linux, Python 3 and the root user (it starts as root only to build the jail, and drops to an unprivileged user before it runs the shell). The helper refuses to run a shell as the server's own user or as root, whatever it is asked: any mode other than exactly `jail` or `plain` is refused, and so is a run that is not root (a message goes back through a pipe of its own, never through the shell's terminal). How the jail is built, in this order: new namespaces for the file system, processes, network, host name and shared memory; a fresh memory-backed root; the system's programs bind-mounted read-only (every mount nested below them read-only too); **`pivot_root` into that root and the old root unmounted** (not `chroot`: a `chroot` can be escaped by a process that is allowed to make a new user namespace or to `chroot` again); all capabilities dropped (including the bounding set, so none can come back); `no_new_privs` set and read back; the resource limits set; and last a **system-call filter (seccomp)** that makes `unshare`, `setns`, `mount`, `umount`, `pivot_root`, `chroot`, `ptrace`, `clone` with any namespace flag, the new mount calls, `bpf`, `keyctl`, `perf_event_open` and `io_uring` fail, and `clone3` fail (so a new namespace of any kind cannot be made), before the shell starts. A filter that does not fit the machine's architecture kills the shell rather than letting it run unfiltered. The shell:

| It can | It cannot |
| --- | --- |
| run as an unprivileged user that is **its own for this terminal** (a random number from 60000 to 64999 that no other running terminal uses, never root; the helper refuses a user id of 0), so one terminal's process limit never starves another user's | become root, keep any capability (`CapEff` is all zeros), gain privileges (`no_new_privs`), or make a user namespace, a chroot, a mount or a trace of another process (a test runs `unshare -Ur` and a chroot escape inside the jail and expects both to fail) |
| read and write its own memory-backed `/work` folder and `/tmp`; **read** the system's programs (`/usr`, `/bin`, `/lib`, mounted read-only; they are readable by it, so it can read any file in them, **including `/usr/local`: keep secrets, keys and deployments out of `/usr/local` and the other system folders on a host that turns the terminal on**; the root holds nothing else of the server's) | see the server's files, the home folder, `/proc/1/environ` or any environment variable of the server (the root is a fresh `tmpfs`) |
| see its own processes | see or signal the server's processes (own process namespace, `/proc` mounted with `hidepid=2`) |
| use the loopback address that is down | reach any network: a new network namespace has no interface, no route, no DNS (no egress at all) |
| use a bounded amount of memory (an address-space limit of 1 GiB, and, where the machine lets the server make one, a control group with 512 MiB of memory, no swap, 128 processes and one CPU), processes, open files and file size (resource limits) | outlive the terminal: when the socket closes the helper's input closes, and the shell and everything it started are killed. If the server itself dies, the kernel kills the helper, and the helper's own parent-death signal takes the shell; a shell that ignores the hang-up signal is killed anyway. The output is read again before the kill, so a shell stuck on a full pipe cannot hold the terminal open |

The control group is best effort and **silent**: where the machine does not allow it (no cgroup v2, or no right to make one), the only memory bound is the address-space limit of 1 GiB for each process times the process limit of 128, and there is no CPU bound but the 900 second CPU-time limit; nothing on the page says so. A host that needs the hard memory cap must provide cgroup v2 to the server. **The random user numbers (60000 to 64999) overlap the range systemd gives to `DynamicUser` services (61184 to 65519). That matters in plain mode only: there the shell runs on the real file system as that number and could share it with such a service. The jail is unaffected (other processes are not visible in it, and its file system is its own).** Pin a number outside the range with `MANYTHREADS_TERMINAL_UID` / `_GID` where this matters. The terminal folder (the *root*) is made by the server with mode `0700` (`0711` in plain mode), is checked every time (a real folder, owned by the server's user, not writable by anyone else, never a link) and each terminal gets a folder inside it made by `mkdtemp` (a random name, so it cannot be guessed ahead). The default root is `<system temp>/manythreads-terminal-<user number>`.

The folder is removed when the terminal ends, and a sweep removes any folder a crashed server left behind (only folders it can prove it made: a session id plus the random part as the name and a marker file in the root). **The terminal is a single-process feature**: the sweep and the list of running shells are the server process's own, so a second server process on the same database would close the first one's open terminals as lost. Run one.

**Where the jail cannot be built** the terminal is **off and says why in plain words** (`GET .../terminal` answers `available: false` and a `reason`). It is also off unless the server is told to allow it. Settings (all read each time they are used):

| Variable | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `MANYTHREADS_TERMINAL_ENABLED=1` | Switches the feature on. Off by default. |
| `MANYTHREADS_TERMINAL_ISOLATION=none` | Plain mode: no namespaces, no read-only system, no network isolation. Only an unprivileged user in a folder of its own, **and it needs the root user too** (the helper refuses to start the shell as the server's own user). It still has the system-call filter and drops every capability. Documented below as the weaker mode. Anything else keeps the jail. |
| `MANYTHREADS_TERMINAL_UID` / `_GID` | Pin the shell's user (must be above 0). Left unset, every terminal gets a user number of its own from 60000 to 64999. |
| `MANYTHREADS_TERMINAL_ROOT` | Where the session folders are made (default a `manythreads-terminal-<user number>` folder under the system temp directory; it must be a real folder of the server's user that nobody else can write to, or the terminal is unavailable). |
| `MANYTHREADS_TERMINAL_IDLE_SECONDS`, `_MAX_SECONDS`, `_WAITING_SECONDS`, `_OUTPUT_BYTES_PER_SECOND`, `_OUTPUT_BYTES_TOTAL`, `_INPUT_BYTES_TOTAL` | Can only **lower** the built-in limits below, never raise them. |
| `MANYTHREADS_TERMINAL_PYTHON` | The Python program to use (default `python3`). |

An availability probe builds a real sandbox, runs `exit` in it and reports the result (cached for 60 seconds), so "available" means a shell really started.

### The credentials are revoked the moment the terminal opens

Opening a terminal opens a sandbox run through the gateway-mcp provider (`McpToolsProvider.openTerminalSandbox`, `packages/plugins/gateway-mcp/src/sandbox.ts`), which **closes it at once**, and closing a run revokes every token minted for it (`app.mcp_run_tokens.revoked_at`). Before it answers, the function checks three things and throws if any fails (so no terminal is opened): no token row of the run is still live, the run is not still `running`, and the token that was minted is refused by `resolve`. The shell therefore holds **no credential at all**, and the run's own endpoint refuses the token it was given. The sandbox run has **no asker**, so no person is named on a run the person did not make. The test (`gateway-mcp/test/terminal-sandbox.test.ts`) keeps the minted token and tries it afterwards, against the service and against the real endpoint; each of the three checks is broken on its own (a `close` that stops the run but leaves a token row live; one that revokes the tokens but leaves the run running; one that does both but a token that still resolves) and each fails closed, with a control that passes. The session row records how many credentials were revoked and the page says so.

### Every session is recorded

`app.terminal_sessions` (`workshop/0005_terminal_sessions.sql`; row policy: a workspace admin reads, nobody but the system writes after the first insert; the database itself refuses a `run_id` that is not a rehearsal run of the same bot, by a trigger, which also refuses a bot filed under another team's or workspace's name; deleting a bot or a team **keeps** the record, with `bot_id` / `team_id` emptied and the bot's name left in `bot_slug`) keeps who (`admin_id`), which bot and sandbox run, when it started and ended, why it ended (`end_reason`), how many bytes went each way, how many credentials were revoked, and the **transcript**. The transcript is **what the shell printed** (its echo of what was typed included), **never the raw keystrokes**. It is turned into plain lines of text (colours, cursor moves and titles removed; `TerminalText` in shared, the same code the page draws with), passed through the existing data-protection redaction (`ctx.hooks.runPreEgress`, destination `terminal`, the `standard` preset, **fail closed**: if the check cannot run, the part is not stored and the record says so), and cut at 256 KiB with a note. A private key block is held back until it ends, so it is masked whole. The record is written in steps while the terminal is open, so a crash loses little. An admin can list earlier terminals of a bot and read a record on the page; a member cannot.

The events `workshop.terminal.opened` and `workshop.terminal.closed` carry no transcript. The audit log shows both as `terminal.opened` / `terminal.closed` (category bots), naming the admin.

### The limits

All are hard, enforced on the server, and the same in the contract tests: **idle** 300 seconds without a key (the output of the shell does not count); **longest** 30 minutes; **output** read from the shell at no more than 128 KiB per second (a shell that prints more is slowed down by pausing it, in 100 ms windows, so the slowing is approximate) and no more than 8 MiB in all (then closed); **input** at most 1 MiB in all, one frame at most 4096 bytes (a larger paste is cut into frames by the page); **one open terminal per admin** (a partial unique index, so two requests at the same moment cannot both win; the second answers `409`); a terminal that was asked for and never connected expires after 60 seconds; a frame that is not valid JSON, binary or of the wrong shape closes the socket.

### The socket, and who may connect

`GET .../terminal/:id/socket` is an ordinary plugin socket route (`ctx.http.route({ ws: true })`) on the app's existing transport (the same cookie session, the same `/api` proxy). The server does three things for **every** plugin socket, which this task added (see [security.md](./security.md)): it refuses an `Origin` whose host differs from the request's host with `403`; it runs the route's `guard` as the caller **before** the upgrade, so a refusal is a real HTTP status (`403` for a non-admin, `404` for another admin's terminal or another tenant's bot, `409` for one that is not waiting); and it registers the socket with the session so signing out closes it. A connection is accepted **once**, for the admin who asked for it, and the claim is a single atomic update. **Signing out ends the terminal within about 15 seconds, not at once**: the socket is closed by the server's revalidation of every plugin socket every 15 seconds (and by sign-out itself when the sign-out reaches this server), and the 10 second role watch below is a second net. A socket opened with a bearer token instead of the cookie session has no session to end, so only the role watch and the limits cover it. When the sign-in behind the socket ends the platform closes the socket and tells the route first, so the record says `session_ended`, not that the page was closed. Every ten seconds a watcher checks the admin is still an admin (`session_ended`) and that the record is still open (a record closed by someone else ends the shell, `server_stop`). The route's `guard` runs through the same plugin-transaction wrapper as a handler. A refusal that does not depend on the machine (a run that is a real run, or another bot's) comes before the check that the terminal can run here, so it is a `403`, not a `409`. The server also revalidates the session of every plugin socket every 15 seconds.

### The page

A Terminal view, not a terminal emulator: it shows the shell's lines as plain text in an output area (`role="log"`), takes a line at a time in an ordinary input (a keyboard, a phone keyboard and a screen reader all work with it), and has buttons for **Send**, **Stop the running command (Ctrl+C)**, **End the terminal** and **Back**. Ctrl+C with nothing selected and Ctrl+D on an empty line work in the box; the up and down arrows bring back earlier lines. It does not run full-screen programs (an editor, `top`): the output is lines, not a screen. It says in plain words what the sandbox is, that it has no network, that the keys were switched off, and what the limits are. The transport is an interface: a WebSocket in the real app, a scripted pretend shell in the mock mode and the tests. Leaving the page closes the socket, which kills the shell.

### Known limits (not hidden)

* The shell runs **on the server host**, not in the bot's real placement. A bot whose behaviour depends on its real pod or runner cannot be debugged here. A dedicated terminal pod is a possible later step.
* **The shipped container image cannot run the terminal today**: it runs as an ordinary user without Python 3. The terminal is off there and says why; turning it on needs a root-capable host with Python 3 and namespaces.
* **Plain mode** (`MANYTHREADS_TERMINAL_ISOLATION=none`) gives an unprivileged user in a folder of its own, but **no network isolation, no process isolation and a visible host file system** (it needs the root user to switch to that unprivileged user; as any other user the helper refuses). The parent-death signal ends its shell if the server dies. It is for a machine that cannot make namespaces; it is not a boundary.
* **An admin who attaches by `runId` ends that run**, including another person's live rehearsal of the bot (by design: the credentials must be revoked first). **The record is the screen text, not the keystrokes**, so a program that overwrites its own line (a carriage return, an erase) hides the earlier output from the record (by design: the record is what a person saw). The cursor moves of the text builder reach at most 8 cells past the written text, so the record never holds thousands of blanks made by one code.
* **The system's programs are readable from the jail** (`/usr`, `/bin`, `/lib`), by design: a shell needs them. Nothing else of the host is in it. Where the jail starts a shell, the memory/CPU control group is best effort (above).
* The jail is a Linux namespace jail, not a virtual machine: it shares the host's kernel.
* Only the shell's output is recorded; what is typed without being echoed (a password prompt) is not in the record, by design.
* The retention plugin does not sweep terminal records yet.
* The slowing of fast output is per 100 ms window, not exact.
* The real-shell tests are skipped on a machine that is not root or has no Python 3 (and say so).

## Decisions the owner should know about

* **The terminal (P7-09) runs on the server host, in a per-session jail, and is off until the server is told to allow it.** A rehearsal has no filesystem of its own, so the sandbox is a new empty folder and an isolated shell, not the bot's real pod or runner. It needs the root user, Python 3 and Linux namespaces; the shipped container image has none of them, so the terminal is off there (with the reason shown) until a host or a dedicated terminal pod is chosen. See [the terminal](#the-terminal-into-a-sandbox-p7-09).
* **Terminal records are admin-only and are not swept by retention yet.** Each holds the shell's output (secrets masked, 256 KiB at most) with the admin's name; the owner should decide how long they are kept.
* **Two server-wide changes came with the terminal.** Every plugin socket now refuses a foreign `Origin` and is closed when its session ends, and a route may declare a `guard` that runs before the upgrade. The live page editor uses plugin sockets too; it is covered by the same checks ([security.md](./security.md#plugin-sockets-origin-session-and-guard)).
* **`TEAM.md` keys.** `defaults.botRuntime` (a list of `shell`, `files`, `browser`; absent means none) and `defaults.guardPreset` (absent means `standard`) are new. A team file without them reads as the defaults above; a file that has them but cannot be read reads as the strictest, never the loosest. Nothing else reads them yet; editing them is a lead's change through a proposal.
* **Skills path.** New skills are filed at `skills/<slug>/SKILL.md`, the path of the spec, and BOT.md names them in `skills: [slug]`. A bot's skills have no runtime reader yet.
* **A new bot is an admin's approval.** A member or lead can run the whole flow; the proposal waits for a workspace admin ([proposals.md](./proposals.md)).
* **Live shows the run's references.** `run_detail` and `recent_runs` return what a run read as a kind and a reference (`run_source_log.source_ref`), never message text, through the asker's own row policy. A reference can name something (a page title or path) the asker may not have been shown in that form. Left as it is for now; the owner should decide whether references are shown to everyone who can see the run or only their kinds.
* **Replay is a re-evaluation of recorded decisions, not a re-run.** Gate and guard lines are replayed against today's list and preset; the local model's call, the data-loss content scan, budgets and rate limits cannot be asked again for free and are reported as such. Messages are read as they are now (an edited or deleted message changes the answer; one the viewer cannot read is not checked).
* **A lead cannot replay another person's private conversation runs.** That is the existing `bot_runs` policy and the replay keeps it.
* **The cost line goes to every lead**, not only to admins, as a sum of the original run's own numbers; the per-line ledger stays admin-only.
* **Two new doors for plugins.** `ctx.capabilities.preview` (the broker's decision with no audit, refused in the restricted doors, and gated by the manifest door `capability.call`: a plugin that does not extend it is refused before the broker is reached; it reads grants only) and `ctx.runs.logDecision`; and one new route option, `readOnly`, which makes the server open the transaction `BEGIN READ ONLY`.
* **The brief is stored** on the session row, visible only to its owner (row policy `owner_id = actor`), with the conversation. The system reads it only for the job. It is not in the audit log, the events or the memory feed.

## Not built

* **Replaying many runs with a model at once.** The full replay is one run at a time, a lead's, behind the estimate and a confirmation. A batch, a schedule and a comparison of the new answer with the old one are not built.
* **Replay of the local model's topic check, of the data-loss content scan and of per-detector choices**, and of runs made before `run_decisions` existed (they have no lines; the page says so).
* **Connection re-assessments.** The reasons exist; nothing files them until Phase 8 has connection events.
* A list of earlier *setup* conversations on a bot's page. The Workshop lists its own conversations (Build, Rehearse, Live) per mode.
* **The terminal (P7-09) is not in a bot's real placement.** It is a throwaway shell on the server host; there is no terminal into a Hermes pod, a team pod or a runner (those are production placements and are refused on purpose). No full-screen programs, no file upload or download, no shared or watched sessions, no replay of a record as a recording, no retention sweep of records, and it is off in the shipped container image.
* Rehearse with the bot's real **Hermes pod or Claude runner**: a rehearsal runs the bot's instructions on its model through the model gateway, so a bot that needs a shell or a browser can only be rehearsed for what it would *say*, not for what those tools would do.
* **Live reads the run records, not the admin audit table.** Anything the audit table holds that a run record does not is not in a Live answer.
* **Connections.** The helper reports that none exist; "Connect Gmail now" of the mockup waits for the connections phase.
* **Seeding from a team template** ("Start from a template" in the mockup): the helper sees the team's bots and may copy their style, but no template is offered.
* The audit entry "setup thread linked from Audit" and a `workshop.*` event: the proposals' own events (`proposals.proposal.created`, `.applied`) are the record today.
